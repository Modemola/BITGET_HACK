Blackout Desk

01Closure windowWhich venues are open, and for how long?

Classifies every hour into one of four regimes from the NYSE and CME Globex calendars, then counts the hours until each venue reopens.

Tells you how long your position is quoted by a single venue before you can act on it again. Every other number here is conditioned on it.

02Premium verdictIs the premium in front of me tradeable?

Splits the closure of the rToken–fair value gap into the leg you can trade and the leg you cannot, then nets the result against costs.

Almost always answers no, and shows the working. This is the one panel built to argue with you rather than agree.

03Drift distributionHow far does price typically travel?

Empirical distribution of divergence by elapsed blackout hour, drawn as quartile and 5–95 bands rather than a single line.

Lets you size against the spread of outcomes instead of the average, and the band widens visibly as the window runs on.

04Historical analoguesWhat happened in weekends like this?

Weighted nearest-neighbour retrieval over past windows, matched only on features knowable before the close so nothing leaks from the future.

Returns a distribution of real outcomes you can read yourself, including the worst point each one reached, not just where it ended.

05ExposureWhat is my book carrying through it?

Breaks your position down by regime across the coming window and prices the typical, 95th-percentile and worst observed move against it.

Turns the percentages above into your own dollars. Type a real position and every figure below recomputes.

06Scheduled eventsIs anything scheduled to explain a move?

Checks FOMC decisions, parsed from the Federal Reserve's own calendar, against the window you are about to hold through.

Establishes what weekend risk is not: across every blackout in the sample, no scheduled rate decision has ever landed inside one. The panel itself carries the count.

07Hedge menuWhat can I trade, and at what cost?

Prices the instruments still quoting while equities and futures are shut, reporting the rolling range of each correlation beside its mean.

Shows why the obvious hedges do not hold: every candidate here changes sign somewhere in the sample, and the cost is stated in dollars.

Before you trade the premium

That weekend premium is not free money.

Across — weekend blackouts, the gap between rToken and fair value closed almost entirely because the reference caught up once futures reopened, not because the token corrected. The token leg, the only one you can actually trade, contributes almost nothing. Fading it is a coin flip that loses to fees.
